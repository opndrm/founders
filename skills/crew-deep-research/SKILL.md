# Crew Deep Research

## Define one research mission

Before searching, identify the decision or question the research will inform.
State a concise scope containing:

- the main question;
- three to six sub-questions when the topic is broad;
- the time period, geography, audience, or constraints that matter; and
- the desired output: brief, comparison, recommendation, or report.

Do not turn one request into unrelated research tracks. Ask for clarification only
when the missing decision would materially change the research.

## Gather evidence

Use $crew-agent-reach for Internet and platform routing. Prefer primary and
authoritative sources: official documentation, filings, laws, research papers,
direct product information, or a first-party statement. Use independent
reporting for context and label it accordingly.

For claims that may have changed, verify them with current sources. Keep a
record of each source used and the specific claim it supports. Do not treat a
search-result snippet as evidence.

## Analyze honestly

- Distinguish established facts, informed inferences, and unresolved questions.
- Compare options against the user’s stated criteria rather than a generic score.
- Identify disagreements between credible sources instead of silently choosing one.
- Do not claim to have verified information that a source does not support.

## Deliver a useful result

Lead with the answer or decision-relevant takeaway. Then provide the supporting
evidence, material tradeoffs, and the remaining uncertainty. Link every source
near the claim it supports. Keep quotations short and use paraphrase by default.

## Boundaries

Research is read-only. Do not purchase, publish, submit forms, change accounts,
or contact people as part of a research task unless the user separately and
explicitly authorizes that action.
